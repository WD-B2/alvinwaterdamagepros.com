Water Damage Restoration in Liverpool, TX

Liverpool is a small city. The 2020 census counted 475 residents, and the city covers about one square mile. It was established in 1837 and sits at an elevation of around 20 feet. Chocolate Bayou forms its eastern border, and the Union Pacific Railroad and County Road 171 run through town. With a bayou on one side and low ground all around, water is a practical concern.

Small Town, Real Water Risks

Bayou overflow

Chocolate Bayou runs along the eastern edge of the city. When it rises, nearby properties can see water.

Low elevation

At about 20 feet above sea level, the town drains slowly after heavy rain.

Older housing and outbuildings

Older homes can have aging plumbing, water heaters and roofs.

Rural distances

Properties along county roads may be some distance from town services.

After a storm, see storm water damage cleanup. If water came up from the ground or in through doors, see flood damage cleanup.

If Water Is Still Spreading, What to Do First

  • Shut off the main water valve, or the valve at the fixture that is leaking.
  • Turn off breakers to wet rooms and stay out of standing water near outlets.
  • Lift furniture off wet floors and move valuables to a dry room.
  • Photograph the damage, then call the 24/7 line and give your Liverpool address.

Insurance and Floodplain Questions

Because Liverpool is close to a bayou, check your flood zone on the FEMA flood map or ask the county floodplain administrator. The Texas Department of Insurance says standard homeowners policies generally do not cover flood, and that flood claims under the National Flood Insurance Program must be filed within 60 days.

Preparing a Small-Town Home for Storm Season

Preparing before a storm is the cheapest protection a Liverpool homeowner has. Photograph each room and your belongings. Know where the main water valve and the breaker panel are. Raise stored items off the floor of sheds and garages. Keep gutters clear so roof water leaves the house quickly. Confirm what your homeowners policy says about wind and rain, and check whether you carry a flood policy.

When Roads Are Closed

Storms can close County Road 171 and low-water crossings. If you are cut off, tell the dispatcher so the crew can plan an alternate route. Meanwhile, shut off the water, turn off the power to wet rooms and take photos. Do not try to cross flooded roads, since moving water is more dangerous than it looks.

  • Keep a battery phone charger so you can reach the dispatcher.
  • Move your vehicle to higher ground before water arrives.
  • Check on neighbors, especially older residents.

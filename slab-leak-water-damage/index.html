Many homes in Alvin are built on concrete slabs, with water lines running beneath or through the foundation. When one of those lines leaks, water has nowhere obvious to go. It moves through the concrete, along the bottom of walls and under flooring. By the time it shows up inside the house, a lot of material may already be wet.

How to Tell Whether You Have a Slab Leak

Slab leaks are hard to confirm because the evidence is indirect. A single sign may have another cause. Several together are a strong warning. A plumber with leak detection equipment confirms the location, and a restoration crew deals with the water that has already spread.

What to Do When You Suspect a Slab Leak

  1. Shut off the main water valve and see whether the meter stops. If it does, the leak is on your side.
  2. Walk the house barefoot and note warm or damp spots. Mark them with tape.
  3. Move rugs and furniture off wet areas and run a fan if it is safe.
  4. Do not drill or cut into the slab yourself.
  5. Photograph all signs, including the meter reading and the bill history.
  6. Call a leak detection plumber for location and us for drying.

What We Do After the Leak Is Located

  1. Map the moisture

    Meters and thermal imaging show how far water spread through flooring and wall bottoms.

  2. Remove wet materials

    Carpet, pad and damaged flooring are lifted. Baseboards and the lower drywall are cut as needed.

  3. Dry the slab and walls

    Dehumidifiers and air movers pull moisture out of the concrete and cavities. Slab drying can take longer than other materials.

  4. Check before rebuilding

    Readings are confirmed so new flooring does not trap leftover moisture.

Slab Foundations in the Alvin Area

Slab-on-grade construction is common in Gulf Coast communities because of flat land and a high water table. A slab leak is not a rare event here. Pipes cast into or run beneath the slab can corrode or shift as the ground moves with wet and dry seasons. The location of the leak matters. A leak under a bathroom behaves differently from one under a kitchen or hallway.

Because the source is hidden, slab leaks are often found late. That is relevant for insurance. The Texas Department of Insurance says gradual leaks and seepage are generally excluded from homeowners coverage. Some policies cover the water damage from a sudden slab line break and the cost of accessing the line, while others do not. Read your policy and ask your agent.

Living Through a Slab Leak Repair

Repairing a slab leak is disruptive. The plumber may tunnel under the slab, break through it or reroute the line through the walls or attic. Each option has a different cost and different impact on your floors. Ask your plumber to explain the options and the effect on flooring. Ask your insurer in advance whether it covers the access work.

The industry reference behind slab leak water damage work is the IICRC S500 standard for professional water damage restoration, which we use as the yardstick when explaining drying goals and equipment.

Do you find the leak?

A licensed plumber with leak detection tools finds and repairs the pipe. We handle the water damage.

Can a slab leak cause mold?

Yes. Moisture under flooring and in wall bottoms can lead to mold if not dried.

Is a slab leak covered by insurance?

It depends on the policy and whether the break was sudden. Ask your agent and keep records.

How long does the slab take to dry?

Often longer than the rest of the structure. Readings decide.

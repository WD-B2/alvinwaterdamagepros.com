A wet ceiling is one of the most common water damage calls in Alvin, and it is one of the more urgent ones. Drywall holds water like a sponge. When it gets heavy enough, it can bulge and fall. Water above it also soaks insulation, which holds moisture against the framing long after the leak stops.

Where Ceiling Water Comes From

Roof leaks

Missing shingles, cracked flashing and worn vents let storm rain into the attic. This is the most common cause after heavy rain.

Upstairs plumbing

Tub overflows, toilet supply lines and shower pans leak through the floor above into the ceiling below.

AC drain lines

A clogged condensate line can overflow into the attic. See AC leak water damage.

Attic pipes

Supply lines in the attic can split from age or freezing and drip onto the ceiling below.

If the Ceiling Is Sagging, Do This Now

  1. Move people and furniture out of the room. A heavy wet ceiling can fall.
  2. Place a bucket under active drips and turn off the light fixture and breaker if water is dripping near it.
  3. If safe, poke a small hole at the lowest point of the bulge to drain the water into a bucket. Stand to the side.
  4. Shut off the main water valve if the source is plumbing.
  5. Photograph the ceiling, the stain edges and the room before cleanup.
  6. Call us for water removal and drying and a roofer or plumber for the source.

Do not run a ceiling fan or turn on lights beneath a wet ceiling. Water in a fixture box is a shock and fire risk.

How We Dry and Repair a Wet Ceiling

  1. Find the extent

    A moisture meter and thermal camera show how far the water spread above and beside the visible stain.

  2. Open it up

    Wet drywall is cut away so insulation and framing can dry. Wet insulation is removed.

  3. Dry the cavity

    Air movers and dehumidifiers dry the joists and the space above. Readings are recorded daily.

  4. Hand off to repair

    Once dry, the ceiling is ready for new drywall, texture and paint. The roof or plumbing repair is done by a licensed trade.

Roof Leaks and Alvin Storms

Alvin gets intense rain. The National Weather Service recorded 33.60 inches at a gauge 1.6 miles southwest of the city during Harvey in 2017. A roof that held up for years can start leaking when it is hit by wind-driven rain in a tropical storm. Water often enters at flashing or a vent and travels along the decking before it shows up as a stain several feet away. The stain is rarely under the leak.

That is why a ceiling stain should be traced before it is painted. Painting over a wet ceiling traps moisture and hides a leak that is still active. If the stain came from storm rain and other rooms are wet, see storm water damage cleanup.

What Not to Do With a Wet Ceiling

Do not cover a stain with paint, do not leave a bulge to see if it dries, and do not climb into an attic with wet insulation and no light. Each of these makes things worse. Call, photograph and keep out from under the wet area.

Ceiling Water Questions

Is emergency ceiling water damage repair available in Alvin?

Yes. Call (844) 950-6900 at any hour. A sagging or dripping ceiling is a priority call, so tell the dispatcher what you see.

Is it safe to stay in a room with a stained ceiling?

If the ceiling is flat and not dripping, usually yes. If it bulges, sags or drips, leave the room and call.

Does insurance cover a roof leak?

Many policies cover sudden roof damage from storms. Wear, age and neglect are usually excluded. Ask your insurer and photograph the stain and the roof.

Can I just paint over the stain?

No. Paint hides the stain but not the moisture. Dry the ceiling and fix the source first.

What if the stain keeps coming back?

The leak is active or the cavity never dried. Have the source traced and the cavity checked with a moisture meter.

A burst pipe is a plumbing failure first and a water damage problem second. A plumber repairs the pipe. A restoration crew deals with the water the pipe released. In most Alvin homes you need both, and the order matters. The water has to be stopped before anything else can be dried.

Signs a Pipe Has Burst Behind the Wall

Not every burst announces itself with a spray. A pinhole or a split at a joint can run for days inside a wall, attic or slab. These are the signs worth acting on.

Sound of running water

A hiss or trickle when every fixture is off points to a leak on the supply side.

Water meter keeps moving

Turn off all fixtures and watch the meter. Movement means water is going somewhere.

Stains and bubbling paint

Brown rings, peeling paint or soft drywall mean water has been present for some time.

Warm or damp floor spots

On a slab, a warm patch can point to a hot water line leaking underneath.

What to Do Before the Crew Arrives

  1. Close the main shutoff valve. Know where it is before an emergency happens.
  2. Open a faucet at the lowest point of the house to drain the remaining water from the lines.
  3. Cut power to the affected rooms at the breaker panel if water is near outlets or light fixtures.
  4. Move rugs, boxes and electronics off wet floors. Place foil under furniture legs.
  5. Take photos or video from several angles, including the failed pipe if you can see it.
  6. Call your plumber for the repair and call us for the water removal and drying.

How We Handle a Burst Pipe Loss

  1. Confirm the water is off

    The crew verifies the source is stopped so the job does not restart with the next pressure surge.

  2. Open and trace

    Moisture meters and thermal imaging follow the water through walls, ceilings and flooring.

  3. Remove and dry

    Standing water is extracted, wet insulation and drywall are cut where needed, and drying equipment is set.

  4. Document for insurance

    Photos, moisture readings and a written scope support the claim for a sudden and accidental loss.

Burst Pipes in Alvin Homes

Homes in Alvin range from older houses near downtown to newer subdivisions, and the plumbing differs with the age. Older homes may have galvanized or copper lines that corrode and split at fittings. Newer homes often use PEX, which can fail at crimped connections or where it was damaged during construction. A crew cannot tell which kind of failure it is without opening the wall, so the scope is confirmed on site.

The cold snap of February 2021 is the best-known local example of pipe failure. The National Weather Service logged extreme cold and an ice storm across the Houston, Brazoria and Galveston zones from February 12 to 17, and many Gulf Coast homes saw lines split. Burst Pipe Questions

Is emergency burst pipe cleanup available in Alvin?

Yes. Call (844) 950-6900 any time. The dispatcher will ask what failed and how much water is present, then arrange a crew as soon as availability allows.

Does homeowners insurance cover a burst pipe?

Typically yes, because a burst is sudden and accidental. Gradual leaks and seepage are generally excluded. Document everything and report the loss promptly so the adjuster can inspect.

Do I need to cut open the walls?

Often a small cut or a few drilled holes are enough to check and dry a wall cavity. Wet insulation and soft drywall usually have to come out.

Should I wait for the adjuster before cleanup begins?

No. Stop the damage now and keep records. Insurers expect you to prevent further loss. Keep damaged materials or photograph them so the adjuster can see them.

How long until the house is dry?

Most pipe losses need three to five days of equipment time. Readings on the framing decide when it is done.

Household appliances use pressurized water lines that are easy to forget about. The hose behind a washing machine, the supply line to a refrigerator ice maker and the dishwasher inlet all sit out of sight. When one fails, water runs until someone notices, and it often notices itself as a stain on a ceiling below or a swollen cabinet.

Washer hoses are a leading cause because they stay pressurized all the time. Braided steel hoses last longer than rubber ones. Closing the washer valves when you are away takes the pressure off.

Do This If an Appliance Is Leaking

  1. Turn off the water at the valve behind or beneath the appliance, or at the main.
  2. Unplug the appliance if the cord and outlet are dry. If not, turn off the breaker.
  3. Pull the appliance away from the wall carefully to find the source and check behind it.
  4. Use towels and a wet vacuum on shallow water. Lift rugs and move anything that can wick water.
  5. Photograph the model label, the failed part and the water damage.
  6. Call us for drying and call an appliance technician for the repair.

What a Hidden Leak Looks Like

Not all appliance leaks produce puddles. A dripping ice maker line may only wet the subfloor beneath the refrigerator. You may notice only a musty smell, a soft spot in the floor or cabinet doors that stick. Flooring that peaks at the seams near the kitchen or laundry room is a common sign.

Insurance Notes for Appliance Leaks

The Texas Department of Insurance says most homeowners policies cover sudden and accidental water damage and generally exclude gradual leaks. A washer hose that burst is typically sudden. A line that dripped for months is typically not. The policy often covers the water damage but not the repair or replacement of the appliance itself.

Washer Floods on Upper Floors

A washing machine on an upper floor, in a laundry closet or a second-story room, is a special risk. When a hose bursts, water goes through the floor into the ceiling and walls below. It can run into light fixtures and out of recessed cans. If you cannot find the source quickly, close the main.

Rooms below the laundry should be checked for ceiling stains and for water in light fixtures. See ceiling water damage repair for what to do if the ceiling has taken water.

Prevention Checklist for Appliances

  • Replace rubber washer hoses with braided steel hoses every few years.
  • Turn off the washer valves when you go on vacation.
  • Pull the refrigerator out once a year and look for water or kinks in the ice maker line.
  • Test the dishwasher shutoff and look under the machine for moisture.
  • Install a leak sensor and, if you travel often, a smart shutoff valve.

Appliance Leak Questions

Is emergency appliance leak cleanup available in Alvin?

Yes. Call (844) 950-6900 any hour. Tell the dispatcher which appliance failed and where the water has gone.

Can I keep using the washer?

Not until the hose or valve is replaced. Ask a technician to check connections.

How do I know if water is under my flooring?

Raised seams, cupping, a musty smell or a floor that feels soft are signs. A moisture meter confirms it.

Will my insurance replace the appliance?

Probably not. Coverage is usually for the damage, not the failed product.

Do I need to move the refrigerator to dry the floor?

Yes. Drying under and behind it is the only way to check for hidden moisture.

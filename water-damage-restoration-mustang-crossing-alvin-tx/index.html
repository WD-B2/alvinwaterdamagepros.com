Burst Pipes, Appliances and Roof Leaks

Water damage in a residential street like these usually comes from a handful of repeat causes. A short list helps you check your own house.

  • A toilet supply line or sink trap that has loosened.
  • A refrigerator or dishwasher line behind a cabinet.
  • A leak around a roof penetration after a hard rain, which stains the ceiling.
  • A water heater drain pan that filled and overflowed.
  • A clogged AC drain pan that spilled into the attic.

After a hard rain, a brown ring on a ceiling points to the roof. The work is the same as in ceiling water damage repair, and the roof itself is a job for a roofer.

Why the quick call matters. Water that sits in carpet pad, baseboards and wall cavities can begin to grow mold when it stays wet for a day or two. Early extraction keeps more of the structure dry.

How Drying Works in Plain Terms

  1. Check and shut off

    Confirm the source is closed and the power to wet areas is safe.

  2. Pull the water

    Extraction removes standing water so drying can begin.

  3. Measure

    Meter readings show how far the water traveled and which materials are wet.

  4. Dry and recheck

    Dehumidifiers and air movers run until readings reach normal levels.

According to the IICRC S500 standard, drying goals come from dry readings taken on similar materials in the same building. The water extraction page describes the equipment, and the carpet water damage page explains when pad and carpet can be saved.

What to Do With Wet Belongings

Sort items by what they are made of. Hard goods such as plastic bins, glass and metal can be wiped and dried once the water is clean. Upholstery, mattresses and books soaked by clean water have a short window, so photograph them and ask a professional before you throw them out.

Do not carry heavy wet items alone, and do not pile wet fabric in a closed room. Keep paper records, passports and photographs on a dry surface in an air-conditioned room. Ask the provider about drying documents if they are damp.

Water heaters fail in two ways. A tank can rupture and release its contents all at once, or a fitting, valve or drain can leak a little at a time. The sudden failure makes a mess you notice immediately. The slow leak does the worse damage, because it can sit under a heater for months without anyone seeing it.

Where Water Heater Leaks Go

In Alvin, water heaters sit in garages, utility closets and attics. Each location has its own problem. In a garage, water runs across the slab and into adjoining rooms. In a closet, it soaks baseboards and flooring. In an attic, it comes down through the ceiling. A tank in an attic may also have a drain pan that has clogged, so the leak overflows unnoticed.

Tank rupture

The tank corrodes and fails. The entire contents drain out, plus any water from the supply line until the valve is closed.

Pressure relief valve

A leaking relief valve can drip for weeks. Water can soak the floor and the drain pan.

Fitting leaks

Connectors at the top of the heater can seep, wetting the tank, the insulation and the flooring below.

Drain pan overflow

A blocked drain line in the pan sends water onto the floor instead of out of the house.

Steps to Take Right Away

  1. Turn off the cold water supply valve on top of the heater.
  2. Switch off power at the breaker for an electric heater, or set a gas heater to pilot or off.
  3. Do not touch the heater or the wet floor if the breaker is in the same wet area.
  4. Move stored items out of the water and onto a dry surface.
  5. Photograph the heater, the label with age and model, and the water damage.
  6. Call a plumber for the heater and call us to dry the area.

How We Clean Up After a Heater Leak

  1. Remove the water

    Standing water is extracted from the floor, drain pan and surrounding rooms.

  2. Check where it traveled

    Water migrates under walls and flooring. Moisture meters find wet baseboards, cabinet bases and subfloor.

  3. Remove saturated materials

    Wet baseboards, drywall and soaked flooring come out. Clean water losses often allow more to be saved.

  4. Dry and document

    Equipment dries the area. Photos and readings are kept for your insurer.

Insurance and Gradual Leaks

The Texas Department of Insurance says most homeowners policies cover sudden and accidental water damage and generally exclude gradual leaks and seepage. A tank that bursts on a Tuesday is sudden. A fitting that has dripped for six months is gradual. The adjuster looks at how long the leak has been present, so photograph the heater's age label, the rust or staining and the area under it.

Preventing a Water Heater Failure

Water heaters give warning signs before they fail. Rust at the base, moisture around fittings and rumbling noises can all mean the tank is near the end of its life. An anode rod, which protects the tank from corrosion, wears out over time and can be replaced. If your tank is old, replacing it before it fails is cheaper than cleaning up after.

  • Check the manufacture date on the label.
  • Flush the tank once a year if the manufacturer recommends it.
  • Make sure the drain pan has a working drain line.
  • Know where the cold water shutoff is.
  • In an attic, consider a leak sensor and an automatic shutoff.

The industry reference behind water heater leak cleanup work is the IICRC S500 standard for professional water damage restoration, which we use as the yardstick when explaining drying goals and equipment.

Water Heater Leak Questions

Is emergency water heater leak cleanup available in Alvin?

Yes. Call (844) 950-6900 any hour and tell the dispatcher where the heater is and how much water is on the floor.

Will insurance cover the heater itself?

Usually not. Policies often cover the water damage from a sudden failure but not the cost of replacing the worn-out appliance.

How old is too old for a tank?

Many tank heaters last roughly ten to twelve years. Check the label for the manufacture date. Rust near the base or around fittings is a warning sign.

Is it safe to use the house while it dries?

Usually yes, but keep out of the affected room and keep the heater's power off until a plumber approves it.

Do I need to dry a closet floor?

Yes. Water under baseboards and under the closet slab edge stays damp if not dried and can lead to mold.

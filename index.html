Local conditions

Why Water Damage Is a Real Risk in Alvin

Alvin sits in Brazoria County on flat Gulf Coast prairie, and Mustang Bayou runs along the edge of the city. Brazoria County's hazard mitigation plan, adopted in 2018, lists Alvin's annual chance of flooding at 35 percent. The same plan records homes in this area that have taken in five feet of water, and notes that flooding could reach seven or eight feet.

The record rainfall here is well documented. The National Weather Service in Houston reports that Tropical Storm Claudette in July 1979 left an official one-day total of 27.75 inches at Alvin. During Hurricane Harvey in 2017 a gauge 1.6 miles southwest of Alvin collected 33.60 inches over the storm. Rain like that does not stay outside. It pushes under doors, through slab cracks and into attics.

Flat land means water drains slowly after a storm. Many Alvin homes are built on slabs, so a leak inside a wall or under the floor can run unseen for days. That is why structural drying matters as much as pumping out the visible water.

Slab foundations

Water from a pipe or a storm can hide under flooring and inside wall bottoms. Moisture readings find it.

Bayou and ditch flooding

Mustang Bayou and local drainage ditches can rise fast. Neighborhoods near the floodplain see repeat losses.

Hot, humid summers

Wet materials left alone in warm, humid air can grow mold quickly, so drying should start the same day if possible.

Older plumbing and appliances

Supply lines, water heaters and washing machine hoses fail without warning. Many are tucked behind walls or in attics.

Do these first

If Water Is Still Spreading, Start Here

The first hour decides how much of the house needs to come apart later. These steps are safe for most homeowners and cost nothing to do while you wait for a crew.

  1. Stop the source

    Close the main water valve or the fixture shutoff. If the leak is from rain, go on to the next step.

  2. Cut power to wet areas

    Turn off breakers for rooms with standing water. Stay out of water if outlets or cords are submerged.

  3. Photograph everything

    Take wide and close-up photos or video of the damage, the source and damaged belongings before moving anything.

  4. Call the emergency line

    Describe what happened and what is wet. A dispatcher will explain what crews can do and when.

Do not use a household vacuum on standing water, and do not walk through water near electrical equipment. If the water is dark, smells like sewage or came from a flood, treat it as contaminated and keep children and pets out.

How it works

From First Call to a Dry, Documented House

Every job follows the same four stages, though the time spent in each depends on the water source and how long materials stayed wet.

  1. Call and triage

    A dispatcher asks what failed, how much water is present and whether anyone is at risk. Active leaks and sewage come first.

  2. Assess and explain

    The crew checks moisture levels, finds hidden wet areas and explains the scope of work and price before starting.

  3. Extract and dry

    Standing water is removed. Air movers and dehumidifiers dry the structure while daily readings track progress.

  4. Repair and close out

    Damaged drywall, flooring or insulation is removed or replaced, and the file is documented for your insurance claim.

No two water losses are priced the same way, and anyone who quotes a number over the phone without seeing the damage is guessing. These are the factors that move the total up or down.

Insurance

Water Damage and Your Homeowners Policy

The Texas Department of Insurance says most homeowners policies cover sudden and accidental water damage, such as a pipe that bursts. Damage from gradual leaks or seepage, and the mold that follows, is generally not covered. Flood from rising water needs a separate flood policy.

Photograph the damage as soon as it is safe, keep damaged items until the adjuster has seen them, and report the loss promptly. If you hold a National Flood Insurance Program policy, the claim has to be filed within 60 days. The state insurance helpline is 800-252-3439. Our water damage services include the documentation that supports a claim, but we do not decide coverage. Your insurer does.

Common questions

Water Damage Questions From Alvin Homeowners

Is emergency water removal available in Alvin?

Yes. The line is open 24 hours a day, every day. Call (844) 950-6900, describe the water, and the dispatcher will tell you the next step and whether a crew can come the same day.

What should I do before the crew arrives?

Stop the water at the source if you can, turn off power to wet rooms, take photos of the damage and move valuables to a dry area. Do not walk through water near electrical outlets.

Will my homeowners insurance pay for water damage?

Often it covers sudden and accidental damage, such as a burst pipe. Gradual leaks and flooding are usually excluded unless you carry flood coverage. Your insurer decides coverage, and we document the damage for your claim.

How long does drying take?

Many jobs need three to five days of equipment time, but wet framing, hardwood and crawl spaces can take longer. Daily moisture readings, not the calendar, decide when drying is done.

Do you also handle mold?

Yes. Mold that follows water damage is addressed on our mold remediation page. Texas licenses mold assessors and remediators separately, so testing and removal are generally done by different companies.

Do you serve areas outside Alvin?

Yes. We cover Manvel, Liverpool and Pearland, along with other nearby Brazoria County towns. Call and give the address so the dispatcher can confirm.

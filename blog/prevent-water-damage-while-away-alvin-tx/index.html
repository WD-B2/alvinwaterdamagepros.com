Prevent Water Damage While Away From Your Alvin, TX Home

A leak while you are home is a nuisance. A leak while you are gone for a week is a disaster. Ten minutes before you leave can prevent most of them.

Short answer: shut off the main water valve, switch the water heater to vacation mode or off, check the AC drain and have someone look in on the house. Those four steps stop most vacation water losses. Details below.

Why Vacation Leaks Are So Costly

Water running unseen for days soaks everything it touches. Floors, walls, ceilings and the contents of rooms below all absorb it. Many insurers expect a house left empty for a long time to be checked at intervals, and gradual leaks are generally excluded under standard policies. Preventing the leak is far cheaper than disputing the claim.

The Pre-Trip Checklist

  1. Turn off the main water valve. If you want the sprinkler or other lines to run, shut off interior valves instead.
  2. Turn the water heater to vacation or pilot mode, or turn off the breaker for an electric one.
  3. Check under sinks and behind the washer and refrigerator for drips or damp spots.
  4. Inspect washer hoses and replace rubber ones with braided steel lines.
  5. Clear the AC condensate drain and look at the pan. Run the system for a few minutes to confirm water flows.
  6. Clear gutters and make sure downspouts carry water away from the foundation.
  7. Set the thermostat to a steady temperature, not off, since high humidity can promote mold.
  8. Ask a neighbor or friend to check the house and give them your contact information.

Alvin Note

Hurricane season runs from June through November on the Gulf Coast. The National Weather Service in Houston reports 33.60 inches of rain at a gauge 1.6 miles southwest of Alvin during Hurricane Harvey in 2017. If you are traveling during hurricane season, move valuables up off the floor, check your flood coverage and make sure someone can reach your house after a storm.

Winter Trips

Freezes are rare in Alvin but not unknown. The National Weather Service logged extreme cold and an ice storm across the Houston, Brazoria and Galveston zones from February 12 to 17, 2021, and many Gulf Coast homes saw pipes split. If a hard freeze is forecast while you are away, shut off the main, drain the lines if you know how and insulate exposed pipes. See frozen pipe water damage if you come home to a thaw leak.

Smart Devices Worth Considering

  • Water leak sensors under sinks, behind the washer and near the water heater.
  • An automatic shutoff valve that closes the main when it detects a leak.
  • A water meter monitor that alerts you to unusual flow.
  • A smart thermostat or humidity monitor.

These devices are not required, but they send a message to your phone so you can call someone to check. A sensor with no one to respond is less useful than one with a neighbor who has a key.

What to Tell the House Sitter

  • Where the main shutoff valve is and how it turns.
  • Where the breaker panel is.
  • The emergency line, (844) 950-6900, and your insurer's phone number.
  • That they should take photos before touching anything.

A short walk through takes ten minutes. Take a few photos so you have a record of how the house looked before you left. If something is dripping, fix it before the trip.

Appliances and Electronics

Unplug small electronics, and put power strips on surge protectors. Storms can bring power surges and water at the same time. Keep valuables off the floor. If you will be gone through hurricane season, store papers and photos in a waterproof box up high.

Talking to Your Insurer Before a Long Absence

Some policies limit coverage if a house is vacant for a long period. A trip of a few weeks is usually not vacancy, but a move or a long absence can be. Ask your agent whether a long trip affects your coverage and what they expect you to do, such as having someone check the house regularly.

The Plumbing You Cannot See

Not every failure is visible. Lines in the attic, inside exterior walls and under the slab can leak or burst without any sign for days. That is why shutting off the main is the strongest single step. It does not matter where the leak is, since it cannot run without pressure.

If you cannot shut off the main, for instance because it supplies an irrigation system or a medical device, shut off the valves for the most vulnerable fixtures. The washer, the water heater and the dishwasher are the usual culprits. Ask a plumber to install a shutoff on any line that lacks one.

After You Return

Before you unpack, walk through the house. Look at ceilings and floors for stains and soft spots. Turn the water back on slowly and watch for drips at every valve and under each sink. Check the water meter after fifteen minutes with everything off. If the dial is moving, there is a leak.

Vacation Prevention Questions

Should I turn off the main water valve when I travel from Alvin?

Yes for any trip longer than a night or two. It stops most plumbing leaks from running unseen.

Is it safe to turn off the water heater?

Yes. Most have a vacation setting. Check the manufacturer's instructions.

Do I need someone to check the house?

It is a good idea. Many insurers expect homes left empty for long periods to be checked.

What if I come home and find water?

Shut off the main, turn off power to wet rooms, take photos and call the 24/7 line.

Does insurance cover vacation leaks?

Sudden leaks are usually covered. Long, unattended leaks may be treated as gradual. Ask your agent.

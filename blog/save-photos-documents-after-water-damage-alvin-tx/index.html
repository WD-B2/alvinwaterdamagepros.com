How to Save Photos and Documents After Water Damage in Alvin, TX

Paper and photos can often be saved if you act within a day or two. The key is to stop them from drying stuck together and to keep mold from starting.

Short answer: do not let wet photos and papers dry in a clump. Separate what you can gently, rinse off mud with clean water, air dry flat or freeze what you cannot deal with right away, and keep everything out of heat. Mold can start within a couple of days in Alvin's humid air, so speed matters.

First, Make Sure It Is Safe to Handle

If the water was from sewage or flood, wear gloves and wash your hands. Do not bring contaminated papers into rooms where people eat or sleep. Valuable or irreplaceable items are better handled by a conservator, and contaminated items may need professional cleaning.

Photographs

  1. Rinse mud or dirt from the surface with clean, cool water. Do not rub.
  2. Gently separate photos that are stuck together while they are still wet. Do not force them apart if they resist.
  3. Lay photos face up on a clean paper towel, a screen or blotting paper, out of direct sun.
  4. Do not stack them.
  5. If you cannot handle them within a day, put them between sheets of wax paper in a sealed bag and freeze them.
  6. Scan or photograph the ones you save so you have copies.

Wet photographs stick to glass and to each other as they dry. Do not use a hair dryer or place them in the sun. Heat can curl and crack the emulsion.

Documents and Books

ItemWhat to do
Loose papers.Lay flat on towels, change towels as they get wet.
Bound books.Stand them upright with pages fanned, or interleave with paper towels.
Heavy stacks.Freeze them in plastic bags to buy time.
Important records.Photograph, then dry or freeze. Ask the issuer for replacements.
Ink that runs.Handle gently and let dry flat.

Freezing stops mold growth and gives you time. Freeze documents in zipper bags. A conservator can freeze-dry bulk paper. Do not iron wet paper.

Keep important papers, such as deeds, insurance policies and identification, in a waterproof container on a high shelf and store scans in the cloud. The Texas Department of Insurance advises keeping damaged items until the adjuster has seen them, so photograph papers before you discard them.

Mold on Papers and Photos

If mold has started, wear a respirator and gloves, take items outside and brush off dry spores gently. Do not wet moldy paper. Valuable or heavily moldy items need a conservator. Keep moldy items away from clean ones. Small areas of mold are one thing. Widespread growth in the house is another, and our mold remediation page explains the Texas rules.

Other Keepsakes

  • Textiles and quilts: rinse and dry flat out of the sun. Do not wring.
  • Framed art: remove from the frame carefully and dry flat.
  • Digital media: do not plug wet drives or discs into a computer.
  • Wood heirlooms: wipe, dry slowly, avoid direct heat.
  • Metal items: dry fast to prevent rust.

Prevention for the Next Event

  • Scan or photograph important documents.
  • Store originals in a fireproof, waterproof box above floor level.
  • Keep a list of what you own for insurance.
  • Back up your phone and computer to the cloud.
  • Keep valuable photos off the floor and away from exterior walls.

Replacing Lost Records

If documents cannot be saved, many can be replaced. Birth and marriage records come from the county or state vital records office. Deeds can be copied at the county clerk's office. Insurance policies can be reissued by your insurer or agent. Passports and driver licenses are replaced through the issuing agency. Tax records may be requested from the IRS.

Digitizing Before the Next Loss

The most reliable way to protect important papers is to scan them. Use a phone or a flatbed scanner, name the files clearly and store them in a cloud account and on a drive kept elsewhere. Do this for family photos too. A rainy afternoon of scanning is cheaper than a conservator's bill.

Working With a Conservator

For irreplaceable paper, books or art, a professional conservator can freeze-dry, clean and stabilize the items. Ask local libraries, archives or universities for referrals. Conservation is not cheap, so decide which items justify it. Ask your insurer whether valuable items are scheduled on your policy.

What to Do in the First Hour

When you first see wet papers and photos, resist the urge to deal with everything at once. Pick up the most valuable items first. Move them to a dry room, put them on a clean flat surface and keep them out of the sun. Then turn to the rest of the house. A few minutes spent stabilizing the keepsakes lets you focus on stopping the water.

If the building is still taking on water, safety comes first. Do not step into water near outlets. Items can wait a few hours in a cool, dry place. Mold takes longer than that to develop.

Supplies Worth Having Ready

  • Clean paper towels or blotting paper.
  • Wax paper or freezer paper for separating photos.
  • Zipper freezer bags in several sizes.
  • A clean plastic bin with a lid.
  • Soft brushes and clean tweezers.
  • A marker for labeling bags.

A small kit in a closet turns a scramble into a procedure. Add a card with these steps, so anyone in the household can follow them when you are not home.

Guidance from archives and conservators, as well as the IICRC S500 water damage standard, supports the same basic rule: stabilize wet paper quickly and keep it out of heat.

Photo and Document Questions in Alvin

Can I freeze wet photos?

Yes. Freezing buys time and stops mold. Put them in a sealed bag with wax paper between prints.

Should I use a hair dryer on wet papers?

No. Heat can damage emulsion and ink. Air dry flat.

Who can restore valuable items?

A paper conservator. Ask your library or museum for referrals.

Will my homeowners policy replace photos?

Insurance can replace the cost of a print but not a memory. Keep digital copies.

What about wet legal papers?

Photograph them and contact the issuing office for replacements.

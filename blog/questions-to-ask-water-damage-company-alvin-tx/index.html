Questions to Ask a Water Damage Company in Alvin, TX

The right questions in the first phone call tell you more than a glossy ad. Here is what to ask and what a straight answer sounds like.

Short answer: ask whether they are licensed and insured, what the scope and price are before work begins, how they document the job, who does what if mold is involved and how they handle your insurance paperwork. A good company answers plainly and puts the details in writing.

Before You Hire

A water emergency puts you under pressure. Storm weeks bring out contractors who knock on doors and ask for payment up front. Take a few minutes to ask these questions. If a company will not answer them, call another.

Questions About the Company

  • Are you licensed and insured? Can I see proof of insurance?
  • Are the people coming to my house your employees or subcontractors?
  • Do you have a local address and a phone line that is answered at all hours?
  • Can you explain which parts of this job are mitigation and which are repair?

Texas does not have a single license for water damage restoration the way it does for mold. Licensed and insured here means the company carries insurance and holds any trade licenses its work requires, such as plumbing or electrical. Ask what is relevant to your job.

Questions About the Work

QuestionWhat a good answer includes
What will you do first?Stop the source, remove standing water, then measure moisture.
How will you know when it is dry?Daily moisture readings compared to a dry reference.
How many days of equipment do you expect?An estimate with the caveat that readings decide.
What gets removed?A list of materials and why each is not savable.
How will you protect the rest of the house?Containment, floor protection and cleaning.

Questions About Price

Be wary of a company that quotes a single number over the phone without seeing the damage. Ask what is in the scope and what could change it. Hidden damage found after walls are opened can change the cost, and a good company discusses that before extra work begins.

  • Will I get the scope and price in writing before work starts?
  • What is the payment schedule?
  • Do I owe anything if my insurance pays less than the estimate?
  • How are equipment days billed?
  • Are repairs priced separately from drying?

Alvin Note

Texas Department of Insurance guidance says to photograph damage, keep damaged items until the adjuster sees them and report hidden damage within days. Ask any company how it will provide photos, moisture logs and a scope for your insurer. For flood claims under the National Flood Insurance Program, TDI says claims must be filed within 60 days, so ask about turnaround on paperwork.

Questions About Mold

If mold is possible, ask who does what. Texas licenses mold assessors and remediators, and generally the same company may not do both on one project. A restoration company that dries your house is not the same as a licensed mold remediator. A company that offers to test and clean the mold itself, as one project, is not following the usual Texas rule. See our mold remediation page.

Red Flags

  • Pressure to sign an assignment of benefits or contract on the spot.
  • A demand for full payment up front.
  • No written scope.
  • A promise of what your insurer will pay.
  • No moisture meter or no readings.
  • A promise of an exact arrival time during a storm.

For sewage and flood losses, ask how contaminated material is handled and disposed of. See sewage backup cleanup and Manvel for local details.

Questions About Insurance Paperwork

  • Will you provide photos, readings and a scope?
  • Will you talk to my adjuster if I ask?
  • Do you use standard estimating software?
  • What do you need from me?

What Happens After You Say Yes

Once you pick a company, ask what the first visit will include and what you will be asked to sign. Typical first steps are a safety check, a walk through the damage with you, moisture readings, a plan and a written authorization. Read the authorization before you sign. It should describe the work and say what you owe.

  • Ask for a copy of everything you sign.
  • Ask who your point of contact is during the job.
  • Ask how often crews will visit and when equipment will be checked.
  • Ask how you will be told if the scope changes.

Storm Season Contractors

After a major storm, out-of-area contractors often arrive and go door to door. Some are fine and some are not. Be careful with anyone who demands a large payment up front, asks you to sign over insurance benefits without explanation or cannot show proof of insurance. Check the company's address and phone number. A local company with a staffed phone line is easier to reach if something goes wrong.

Checking References and Reviews

Read reviews from more than one source. Look for patterns, such as complaints about communication or surprise charges. Ask for one or two recent customers you can call. A company that works with insurers will also be able to tell you how its documentation is usually received.

What a Good Phone Call Sounds Like

A prepared company asks you questions before it tells you anything. It asks where you are, what happened, whether the source is off, how much water there is and whether anyone is at risk. It explains what it will do first and what you can do while you wait. It does not promise an exact time during a storm. It does not pressure you to sign anything over the phone.

A less prepared company starts with price. It names a number before it has any information, or it pushes you to commit before you have asked a single question. Take that as a signal to keep looking.

Writing Down the Answers

Keep a notepad by the phone. Write down the name of the person, the time and what they told you about arrival, scope and cost. If the company later says something different, your notes help. If you are comparing companies, notes make comparison easy.

Hiring Questions in Alvin

How do I check a company in Alvin?

Ask for proof of insurance, check any trade licenses and read reviews from several sources. Call the references if offered.

Should I get more than one estimate?

If time allows, yes. Water emergencies often do not allow it. Stop the damage first.

Can a company require me to use them for repairs?

You choose your contractor. Ask whether mitigation and repair are separate.

What if the company wants a deposit?

Ask for the reason and the amount in writing. Large up-front payments are a warning sign.

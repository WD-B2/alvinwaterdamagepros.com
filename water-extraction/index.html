What Water Extraction Actually Involves

Water extraction is the removal of standing and saturated water using pumps, wands and extraction machines. It is the first physical step in almost every water loss, and it comes before drying equipment goes in. Fans and dehumidifiers work on moisture in the air and in materials. They are slow and expensive tools for removing a puddle.

On a hard floor, a crew can clear most of the water with a squeegee and wet vacuum. On carpet, the job is harder. Water sits in the pad underneath, and a truck-mounted or portable extractor has to pull it up through the fibers. On a deep loss, a submersible pump moves water out before any wand work starts.

Hard surfaces

Tile, vinyl and sealed concrete are cleared with pumps and wet vacuums, then checked for water that ran under baseboards.

Carpet and pad

Extraction runs over the carpet and the pad is checked. Pad that stayed wet or took contaminated water is usually removed.

Wall bottoms

Water wicks upward into drywall. Moisture readings show how high it traveled and whether cuts are needed.

Cabinet bases

Water collects under toe kicks and inside cabinet bases, where it can sit for weeks without drying on its own.

If Water Is Still Spreading, Do These Things First

The time between the leak and the crew's arrival is when most of the preventable damage happens. These steps are safe for most homeowners.

  1. Stop the source. Close the main valve or the shutoff under the fixture. If rain or flood water is entering, move to the next step.
  2. Turn off breakers to wet rooms. Do not stand in water to reach a panel. If the panel is wet, call the utility.
  3. Lift furniture legs onto foil or blocks so wood and fabric stop wicking water.
  4. Pick up rugs, books and electronics that are still dry and move them out of the room.
  5. Photograph the water, the source and the damage before anything is moved.

Do not run a household vacuum on standing water and do not use a space heater in a wet room. If the water came from a sewer line or a flood, keep children and pets out until it is cleaned.

How Extraction Fits the Rest of the Job

  1. Find the source

    The crew confirms the leak is stopped and identifies the water category.

  2. Remove the water

    Pumps and extractors clear standing water, starting with the deepest areas.

  3. Check what is saturated

    Meters and a thermal camera map water in carpet, pad, walls and subfloor.

  4. Hand off to drying

    Equipment is placed for structural drying, and readings are logged daily.

Extraction in Alvin: What Local Conditions Change

Alvin is flat, and storm water does not drain away quickly. After Hurricane Harvey in 2017, the National Weather Service recorded 33.60 inches of rain at a gauge 1.6 miles southwest of Alvin. Water that deep does not just wet floors, it gets into wall cavities and under cabinets. A crew has to extract at several levels, not just the visible surface.

Many homes in the area are built on slabs. When a pipe fails under a sink or behind a wall, the water spreads across the slab and into every room that shares it. Starting extraction early limits how far it travels. If the water came from outside during a storm, see flood damage cleanup for the contamination steps that apply.

Questions About Water Extraction

Is emergency water removal available in Alvin?

Yes. Our line is open 24 hours a day, every day. Call (844) 950-6900, describe the water and the dispatcher will explain the next step and whether a same-day crew is possible.

Can I extract the water myself?

For a small spill, a wet vacuum works. For more than a thin layer, or any water that reached carpet pad or walls, you will leave moisture behind. That is the moisture that leads to mold and swollen framing.

How long can water sit before it causes lasting damage?

Drywall and carpet pad begin to degrade within a day. Hardwood can start to cup within a few days. Mold growth can begin in the first few days in warm, humid conditions, which is common in Alvin.

Do you remove the carpet?

Sometimes. Clean water caught early may allow carpet to be dried in place. Contaminated water, or pad that stayed wet too long, usually means removal.

Is extraction covered by homeowners insurance?

If the water came from a sudden and accidental source such as a burst pipe, extraction is commonly part of the covered loss. Flooding usually needs a separate flood policy. Your insurer makes the call, so report the loss promptly.

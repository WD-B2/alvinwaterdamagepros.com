A sewage backup comes up through a floor drain, toilet or tub when the line is blocked or overwhelmed. The water contains bacteria and viruses, and it does not become safe because it looks clear. Water that touched a sewer line belongs in the highest contamination category, usually called Category 3 or black water.

How to Recognize a Sewage Problem Before It Floods

Backups often announce themselves. A sewer line that is struggling gives early warnings, and acting on them can limit the damage.

Slow drains throughout the house

If several fixtures drain slowly at once, the main line may be blocked.

Gurgling from drains

Air pushed back through the traps can signal a clog downstream.

Water in a floor drain or tub when you flush

A sign that wastewater is coming back up.

Sewer odor indoors

A smell inside the house means water or gas is entering where it should not.

If Sewage Is Still Coming Up, Do This

  1. Stop using water in the house. No flushing, laundry or dishwasher.
  2. Keep children, pets and anyone with a weakened immune system out of the affected area.
  3. Do not wade through the water barefoot. Wear rubber boots and gloves if you must enter.
  4. Turn off power to the affected rooms only if you can reach the panel safely and dry.
  5. Call a plumber to clear the line and call us for removal and sanitizing.
  6. Photograph the damage before cleanup for your insurer.

Do not use a household vacuum, mop or towels on sewage, and do not try to dry it with fans alone. Fans spread contaminants into the air.

How We Clean and Sanitize

  1. Protect and contain

    Crews wear protective gear and may seal the area so contaminants do not spread.

  2. Remove the water

    Pumps and extractors clear the water. Contaminated waste is bagged for disposal.

  3. Remove porous materials

    Carpet, pad, drywall and insulation touched by sewage are cut out. They cannot be made safe by drying.

  4. Clean, treat and dry

    Hard surfaces are cleaned and treated with antimicrobial products, then structurally dried.

Why Sewage Backups Happen Around Alvin

Heavy rain can overload municipal sewers and force water up into homes. The Brazoria County hazard mitigation plan notes repeated flooding in parts of Alvin, and storm flooding often overlaps with sewer problems. Tree roots, grease, wipes and collapsed older lines cause backups in dry weather too. Ask your city or utility whether the blockage is on your private line or the public main. The answer decides who pays for the repair.

Health and Safety After a Backup

Sewage can carry bacteria, viruses and parasites. Contact with skin, eyes or mouth is the main way people get sick. Wash hands thoroughly after contact. Wear waterproof gloves and boots if you have to handle anything. Keep pets away from the area. If a household member has a weak immune system, is pregnant or is very young or old, have them stay somewhere else until cleanup is done.

Preventing the Next Backup

If the blockage was in your private line, a plumber can camera the pipe to find roots, a belly or a break. Do not flush wipes, even labeled flushable ones, and keep grease out of drains. If the problem came from the public sewer during heavy rain, a backwater valve on your line can keep wastewater from flowing back. Ask a licensed plumber and your city whether one makes sense for your property.

The industry reference behind sewage backup cleanup work is the IICRC S500 standard for professional water damage restoration, which we use as the yardstick when explaining drying goals and equipment.

Is homeowners insurance likely to cover a backup?

Many policies exclude sewer backup unless you added an endorsement. Check your policy and ask your agent.

Can I clean it myself?

Small amounts on hard floors may be handled with proper protection. Anything that reached carpet, walls or HVAC needs professionals.

How do I know if my symptoms are connected?

If someone feels sick after contact, call a doctor. Do not guess.

Will the smell go away?

Once contaminated materials are removed and the area is cleaned, the odor should fade.

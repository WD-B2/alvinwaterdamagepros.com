Leaks That Start Inside the Home

A leak is easy to underestimate in any home. Water travels under flooring and into wall bases before it shows.

  • Washing machine and dishwasher hoses behind appliances.
  • Refrigerator ice maker lines that seep into subfloor or cabinets.
  • A water heater tank that weeps at the base for weeks.
  • A pinhole in a supply line inside an exterior or interior wall.
  • A drain or sewer line that backs up into a tub or floor drain.

For a split supply line see burst pipe water damage. For a hose or ice maker line see appliance leak water damage.

What to Do on Wet Floors Before Cleanup

Treat every wet room as electrically unsafe until power to it is off. Never touch a breaker while standing in water. If a drain or sewer line is the source, keep children and pets away and do not clean it yourself. Sewage water carries bacteria that ordinary fans and mops do not make safe.

Safe first steps. Turn off the water at the shutoff if you can reach it dry. Photograph the source and the damage. Move small valuables to a dry room. Then call (844) 950-6900 and ask about availability.

How Drying Is Done

Extraction comes first, because standing water slows everything else. Technicians then check walls, floors and ceilings with a moisture meter and set air movers and dehumidifiers to match what the readings show. Equipment stays until readings return to normal.

Hardwood, laminate and carpet each respond differently. A wet floor may need baseboards removed to dry the wall base, which is covered on the structural drying page.

MaterialWhat usually happens
Carpet and pad.Pad is often removed, carpet may be dried in place if the water was clean.
Laminate.Swelling at the edges often means replacement.
Drywall.Cut out if it stays wet or has soaked up contaminated water.
Insulation.Wet insulation usually comes out of the cavity.

Insurance Notes

Report the loss promptly and keep damaged items until your adjuster has seen them. Standard homeowners coverage and flood coverage are separate. A flood policy through the National Flood Insurance Program carries a 60 day window to file proof of loss. Your insurer, not a restoration provider, decides what is covered.
